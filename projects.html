Coursework simulation

Earth–Satellite Particle-System Simulation

This MECH 228 coursework exercise used a provided MATLAB framework for a four-particle system. I completed the center-of-mass, momentum, velocity, and position updates, then used an Earth-and-three-satellite model to compare trajectories and linear momentum under different parameter settings.

Numerical model

Pairwise gravitational forces updated momentum at each time step; momentum determined velocity, and velocity advanced each particle's position. The center of mass was recalculated throughout the simulation.

Parameter study

Changing satellite velocity, satellite mass, and Earth's initial velocity showed how orbital behavior and system motion changed while total momentum remained conserved in the closed system.

Simulation workflow

  1. Completed the center-of-mass, momentum, velocity, and position-update sections in the provided MATLAB model.
  2. Ran the Earth-and-three-satellite baseline case and plotted trajectories and momentum components.
  3. Varied initial velocity and mass values, then compared the resulting system response.

Featured project

HarveStar Lunar Harvest End Effector

This Canadian Space Agency project asked our team to design, build, and test an end effector for automated harvesting in a lunar greenhouse. We chose a crab-claw design with one fixed side, one servo-driven side, and elastic gripping bands. I wrote the control program and completed the SolidWorks simulations; a teammate created the CAD model.

End-effector design

The claw kept one side fixed and moved the other with a servo. Elastic bands added compliance so the same mechanism could grip plants with different shapes without adding more moving parts.

Control & validation

The CircuitPython program controlled the pickup path, release motion, speed, grip angle, and emergency stop. SolidWorks simulations were used to check displacement, stress, and likely high-strain areas.

Engineering workflow

  1. Defined the working conditions of a lunar greenhouse, including reduced gravity, limited space, safety, and gentle plant handling.
  2. Compared several gripper ideas and selected the crab-claw concept for its simple structure and adaptable grip.
  3. Used SolidWorks to check stress and displacement under the expected gripping load.
  4. Programmed the path, servo motion, release sequence, and emergency stop, then refined them through physical testing.

Featured project

Water Filtration Prototype

Our team built a working water-treatment prototype that pumped water through alum dosing, magnetic mixing, settling, and multi-layer filtration. I used SolidWorks to plan the layout and wrote the Arduino/C control code independently.

Design logic

The SolidWorks assembly helped us place the pump, base plate, dosing tower, magnetic mixer, filters, and receiving container before we started the physical build.

Control & build

The Arduino/C program coordinated the water-level check, alum dispenser, DC-motor mixer, and pump. I adjusted the timing sequence until the dispenser delivered the intended amount of alum.

Process flow

  1. Pump untreated water into the process structure.
  2. Use the dispenser to introduce alum for coagulation and sedimentation.
  3. Drive a gear-based magnetic system at the bottom so the internal magnetic mixing bar can sync with the changing magnet position.
  4. Pump water onward through multi-layer filtration and collect cleaner output in a final container.

Featured project

Femoral Head Indexing Station

This project redesigned a manufacturing cell so a prosthetic femoral head could move through laser-etching and surface-mapping positions. I helped build the recycled-material transmission, tested the motor, added encoder feedback, and wrote the Arduino/C control code. I also organized the test data in Excel and plotted the results in Python.

Mechanical system

A belt-and-lever transmission converted motor rotation into the required indexing angles while holding the femoral head only along its permitted lower contact band.

Controls & testing

Motor tests established a usable duty-cycle range, while encoder feedback checked position and reduced the effect of belt slip. The program also handled dwell timing and emergency stopping.

Engineering workflow

  1. Defined the required 108° clockwise, 198° counterclockwise, and 90° clockwise indexing sequence.
  2. Recorded motor speed, torque, and duty-cycle data in Excel, then used Python to plot and interpret the results.
  3. Programmed the microcontroller in Arduino/C to control motion, dwell timing, encoder position checks, and emergency stopping.
  4. Adjusted the recycled-material transmission and control settings until the mechanism moved through the sequence consistently.
